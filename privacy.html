Aegida privacy policy

Last updated October 10, 2026

The short version. Aegida keeps what it learns about your phone on your phone. It has no ads, no account and no analytics. The only thing it ever sends to a person is a short message to the one person you choose, and only if you turn that on.

Want to see how Aegida works? Read it in simple words or in detail.

Who makes Aegida

Aegida is a free app made by Kevin Idzi, an individual developer.

What Aegida looks at on your phone

All of this stays on your phone.

  • The alerts that other apps show you. Aegida looks at their title and text for scam links and fake warnings. It does not send what they say. When one looks like a scam, Aegida keeps who it was from and its first words, only on your phone, so it can show you what it was; see below.
  • Which apps open web links, texts and calls, and which apps have notification access, screen control or device-administrator rights.
  • Whether your phone uses a private DNS setting.
  • The list of apps on your phone: each app’s name, which store installed it, and, for apps that did not come with the phone, a code that identifies who made it.

Aegida does not look at the inside of your apps, your contacts, your location, your microphone or your camera. It does not listen to calls or record anything.

What Aegida keeps on your phone

  • Your first name and the picture you chose.
  • The name, email address and phone number of the person you pick to be told (your guardian), and a long random private name used to send them messages.
  • A record of how your phone looked when you set Aegida up, the problems it found, and a 90-day history. For a scam message or a website pop-up, Aegida keeps who it was from, the first words of what it said and when, so it can show you what it was. A link in those words is replaced by “(link)”. This stays on your phone and is never sent. The details of a warning are cleared when you tap “Got it”, and the short History line is kept 90 days.
  • Messages waiting to be sent, and the downloaded list of known scams.

This is kept in Aegida’s private storage. It is left out of cloud backups and phone-to-phone transfers. Uninstalling Aegida removes it.

What Aegida sends, and to whom

  • A short message to your guardian, only if you turn it on. It is a few fixed sentences in your name, such as “A message with a scam link arrived on Ruth’s phone. Aegida warned Ruth.” It never contains the words of your messages or a whole web address. A note about a scam message can name the app it came from, and show a website with most of its letters hidden (the first two letters, stars and the ending, like pa*****.example), so your guardian can tell sites apart. It travels through ntfy.sh, a public message service. The private name is long and random, and only you and your guardian know it. ntfy.sh can see that a message was sent and the internet address it came from, and holds messages for a short time so your guardian’s phone can collect them. ntfy.sh has its own privacy policy. Once ntfy.sh has delivered a message, Aegida cannot take it back.
  • The invitation email. Aegida opens your own email app with the invitation already written. You press send. Aegida does not send it for you.

Aegida sends nothing else itself. It has no ads, no analytics, no crash-reporting service and no accounts. It does not sell or share your information.

The optional website-blocking step

Setup offers to help you set your phone’s “Private DNS” to security.cloudflare-dns.com, run by Cloudflare. It is a setting on your phone, not part of Aegida, and you can skip it or turn it off at any time. Aegida only reads that setting to tell you if it gets turned off; it never sees the websites you visit. While the setting is on, your phone sends the name of each website it looks up to Cloudflare, so Cloudflare can refuse known scam websites. Cloudflare’s own privacy policy covers what it keeps.

What Aegida downloads

Once a day Aegida downloads its list of known scams: two public files from GitHub Pages, which is run by GitHub. The request names the file and nothing else. Nothing about you or your phone goes with it. GitHub may record the internet address that asked, as any website does.

What your phone will ask you to allow

  • Notification access, so Aegida can look for scam links in the alerts on your screen.
  • A list of your apps, so Aegida can notice a new app, or one that lets someone else control your phone.
  • A small notice on your screen, so you can see Aegida is running.
  • Internet, to download the scam list and to send your guardian’s message.
  • Showing notifications and starting when the phone starts, so Aegida can warn you and keep working after a restart.

Your choices

  • You can turn off messages to your guardian at any time in My settings. Aegida then stops sending and cancels the “hasn’t checked in” message that is waiting. A message that could not be sent is kept on your phone, not sent while this is off.
  • You can pick a different guardian at any time.
  • You can take away any permission in your phone’s settings, or uninstall Aegida.

Children

Aegida is made for adults. It is not made for or aimed at children.

If a parent chooses to use Aegida on a child’s phone, the same rules in this policy apply. Aegida’s developer collects nothing about the child. What Aegida keeps stays on the phone. The only message that ever leaves it is the optional short note to the person the parent picks, and it uses only the first name that was typed in.
